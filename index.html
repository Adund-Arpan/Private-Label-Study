Every app decides what you see first. This study asks whether that decision—and the price next to it—changes what ends up in your basket.

Placement is a quiet lever in retail. On a shelf, you can scan the whole aisle. On a phone, someone has already chosen what appears first.

Participants see one of four randomly assigned combinations: the private-label reference or national brand first, with either an equal price or a price difference. The study records the product selected, placement recall, perceived price difference, value perception and switching intention.

This version uses platform-specific product pairs and screenshot-inspired iPhone interfaces. Because categories, pack sizes and brand relationships differ, review the instrument and pilot it before collecting dissertation data.

05 Methodology & sources

Research
references.

Question design and construct mapping are grounded in the studies cited below.

01What each question measures

Standardized/source-based question grounding. Q5–Q7 are source-grounded adaptations of placement, price-gap and value constructs discussed in Long & Amaldoss (2024) and Choi & Turut (2023); Q8–Q9 adapt switching-intention items/frameworks from Bansal, Taylor & St. James (2005). These papers support the underlying constructs and research logic, but the revised items should be treated as adapted items rather than claimed as verbatim validated scales.

  • Q1–Q3 · App use & buying habits. Primary app (also screens for current usage), order frequency and usual pantry-brand type. Standard screening scaffolding; not validated attitude scales.
  • Q4 · Product choice. Observed selection of the private-label reference or national-brand option under the assigned placement and price condition.
  • Q5 · Placement recall. “Which product stood out more on the shelf?” A single categorical check replaces the original two prominence ratings. Inspired by the placement checks attributed to Long & Amaldoss (2024) and Choi & Turut (2023).
  • Q6 · Price-gap check. “I noticed a price difference between the two products.” Retains original item mc3; source attribution from the original instrument: Long & Amaldoss (2024) and Choi & Turut (2023).
  • Q7 · Perceived value. “The price difference made the first reference brand seem like better value.” Retains the original pa2 construct; adapted from Choi & Turut (2023).
  • Q8 · Switching consideration. “I would consider switching from the comparison brand to the first reference brand.” Retains the original si1 construct; adapted from Bansal, Taylor & St. James (2005).
  • Q9 · Trial intention. “I would try the first reference brand on my next quick-commerce order.” Retains the original si2 construct; same switching framework. Product/category wording has been updated.

03What changed in the shorter instrument

The original flow contained six screening items, one product choice and nine reactions (16 questions). This version contains three screening items, one choice and five reactions (9 questions).

Removed: the duplicate current-use question, multi-app checklist, private-label awareness, relative national-brand price rating, replacement intention and repurchase intention. Two placement ratings become one categorical prominence question. No demographics are requested.

Responses preserve legacy keys where the meaning remains compatible. Removed scores are blank, never imputed. The new placement check is stored separately as prominence; it is not disguised as the old mc1/mc2 scores. Pantry habits are stored in usualPantryBrand; the original noodle-specific buying field is left blank. This is a revised instrument and requires measurement review and pilot testing.

04Experiment, brands & data

Conditions. Each respondent is randomly assigned to one of four conditions formed by two factors: placement (private label first vs. national brand first) and price anchoring (equal price vs. national brand priced higher than the private label). Study prices are fixed experimental stimuli and are not based on live retail prices.

Reference pairs. Swiggy: NOICE / Haldiram’s; Zepto: Aaha! / Go Zero; Blinkit: Whole Farm / Rajdhani; Flipkart: pykd / Sunfeast; Amazon: Amazon Basics / Sleepwell. PVT and NAT are legacy reference codes, not verified ownership classifications. Confirm private-label status before analysis. Zepto compares dessert formats; other pairs also differ in size and formulation.

Amazon pending. The supplied screenshot shows Amazon Basics and Kuber Industries, not Sleepwell. Sleepwell remains visibly pending and Amazon response submission is blocked until its image, model, size and reference price are supplied.

Data. Consent, screening and the five reactions are retained. Answers are sent only on Submit to the original Google Apps Script destination. New fields identify the platform, products, individual pack sizes and reference prices. The existing receiver may need changes to store these fields; verify it before launch. Failed or unconfirmed submissions can be downloaded as JSON.

Imagery and interface. Product imagery is cropped from the user’s screenshots. Ratings shown are screenshot references, not live ratings. Interfaces are independent, screenshot-inspired research simulations, not official storefronts. No ordering, account login or payment is provided. The two-product shelf omits unrelated products from the screenshots.
